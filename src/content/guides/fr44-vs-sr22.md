FR-44 and SR-22 are both certificates of financial responsibility, but they aren't the same requirement — and filing the wrong one won't satisfy a court or FLHSMV order. Here's a full comparison.

## What they have in common

Both FR-44 and SR-22 are filed electronically by your insurance carrier to prove you carry required liability coverage. Neither is a standalone insurance product — both attach to a standard auto policy. Both are typically required for a set period, often three years, and both require continuous, uninterrupted coverage.

## Where they differ

| Factor | SR-22 | FR-44 |
|---|---|---|
| Required liability limits | Florida's standard minimums | Higher than standard minimums |
| Common trigger | Uninsured driving, license reinstatement, certain moving violations | DUI/DWI conviction |
| States that use it | Most states use SR-22-equivalent forms | Only Florida and Virginia use FR-44 |
| Typical cost impact | Moderate increase | Larger increase, due to higher limits + risk |

## Why Florida uses FR-44 instead of SR-22 for DUI cases

Florida's legislature set higher required coverage limits specifically for DUI-related cases, and created the FR-44 form to track compliance with those higher limits. Other violations that require proof of financial responsibility, but don't carry the same higher-limit requirement, use the standard SR-22 form instead.

## Which one do you need?

Check your official notice from the court or FLHSMV — it will specify FR-44 or SR-22 by name. If your case involves a DUI or DWI conviction, expect FR-44. If it involves driving without insurance or certain other violations, SR-22 is more common. When in doubt, we can help confirm which applies based on your notice.

## Can you have both at different points?

Yes. Some drivers first need an SR-22 for one violation, and later require an FR-44 if a DUI conviction occurs. Each filing is assessed independently against its own triggering violation and timeline.
