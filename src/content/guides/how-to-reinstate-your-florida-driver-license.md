License suspensions in Florida happen for a range of reasons, and reinstatement steps vary depending on the cause. This guide covers the general process, with a focus on where insurance requirements fit in.

## Step 1: Confirm why your license is suspended

Your suspension notice or the FLHSMV website will specify the reason — commonly DUI conviction, driving without insurance, accumulated points, unpaid tickets, or a court order. The reason determines exactly what's required for reinstatement.

## Step 2: Resolve any outstanding requirements

Depending on the cause, this might include completing a required course (such as a DUI program), paying outstanding fines or reinstatement fees, or satisfying a court-ordered waiting period. Address these before moving to the insurance step, since many reinstatement paths require them first.

## Step 3: Get compliant insurance in place

Many suspensions — especially DUI-related ones — require proof of FR44 insurance before reinstatement. Others, such as driving-without-insurance violations, typically require an SR22 filing instead. Confirm which applies to your case, since filing the wrong one won't satisfy the requirement. Our [FR44 vs. SR22 guide](/guides/fr44-vs-sr22/) explains the difference in detail.

## Step 4: Your insurer files electronically

Once your policy is active with the correct coverage limits, your insurance company files the FR44 or SR22 certificate directly with the FLHSMV. This step is typically automatic — you don't need to submit anything yourself once your policy is active.

## Step 5: Pay the reinstatement fee

Florida charges a reinstatement fee that varies based on the type and number of suspensions on your record. This is separate from any insurance costs and is paid directly to the FLHSMV.

## Step 6: Confirm reinstatement

Once all requirements are met — course completion, fees, and insurance filing — you can confirm your license status through the FLHSMV. Processing times can vary, so confirming before you drive again is worth the extra step.

## Staying reinstated

If your reinstatement involved an FR44 or SR22 filing, remember that maintaining continuous coverage for your full required period (often three years) is essential. A lapse can trigger another suspension and reset your filing period.
