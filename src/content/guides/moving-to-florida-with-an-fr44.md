Moving with an active FR44 or SR22 requirement adds a layer of planning most relocation checklists don't cover. Here's what to keep in mind.

## Moving to Florida from another state

If you have an out-of-state SR22 or equivalent filing and you're relocating to Florida permanently, you'll generally need to establish Florida insurance and, if your underlying violation would trigger an FR44 requirement under Florida law, transition to that filing instead. Because Florida's FR44 threshold is different from most states' SR22 rules, it's worth confirming with the FLHSMV or a Florida-licensed agent exactly what's required once you're a resident.

## Moving within Florida

If you're relocating to a different city within Florida while under an existing FR44 requirement, the filing itself doesn't reset — but you do need to update your address with your insurance carrier promptly. Rates can vary by location, so a move might also be a good moment to compare quotes again.

## Timing the transition carefully

As with any insurance change, the key risk is a lapse in coverage. If you're switching carriers as part of your move — whether from out of state or just for a better local rate — confirm your new policy and filing are active before canceling the old one.

## Documents to have ready

- Your current FR44 or SR22 documentation, including your specific filing period
- Your new Florida address
- Your driver's license (updated to Florida, if you're a new resident)
- Vehicle registration details

## Confirming your new requirement

Because FR44 is specific to Florida (and Virginia), drivers moving from a state that only uses SR22 sometimes assume their existing filing type carries over unchanged. It doesn't automatically — Florida law determines which filing applies based on your underlying violation, not which state originally required it. Confirming this early avoids a mismatched filing that doesn't satisfy the requirement.
