Insurance documents are notoriously hard to parse, full of terms that sound similar but mean very different things. This guide breaks down exactly what's in a Florida auto insurance policy, so you actually understand what you're paying for.

## The two coverages every Florida driver must carry

**Personal Injury Protection (PIP).** Florida is a no-fault state, meaning PIP covers a portion of your own medical expenses after an accident, regardless of who caused it. This is required for every Florida driver.

**Property Damage Liability (PDL).** This covers damage you cause to someone else's property — most commonly their vehicle — in an at-fault accident. Also required for every Florida driver.

## Coverage that becomes required in certain situations

**Bodily injury liability.** Not universally required for every driver by default, but becomes mandatory after certain violations, including FR-44 and SR-22 requirements. See our [Florida insurance requirements guide](/guides/florida-insurance-requirements-explained/) for the full picture of when this applies.

## Optional coverage worth understanding

**Comprehensive coverage.** Pays for damage to your own vehicle from non-collision events — theft, vandalism, fire, flooding, or hitting an animal. Often required by lenders if you're financing or leasing.

**Collision coverage.** Pays for damage to your own vehicle after an accident, regardless of fault. Also commonly required by lenders.

**Uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage.** Protects you if you're hit by a driver with no insurance or insufficient coverage. Insurers must offer this in Florida, though it isn't automatically included — many drivers add it given the number of uninsured drivers on Florida roads.

Our full [Florida auto insurance coverage guide](/florida-auto-insurance/) covers each of these in more depth.

## Understanding your coverage limits

Liability coverage is expressed as three numbers, like 10/20/10 or higher. The first number is bodily injury liability per person, the second is bodily injury liability per accident, and the third is property damage liability — all expressed in thousands of dollars. Our guides on [what 10/20/10 means for SR-22](/blog/sr22-10-20-10-florida/) and [what 100/300/50 means for FR-44](/blog/fr44-100-300-50-florida/) break this down with real examples.

## How FR-44 and SR-22 change your policy

If you're under an FR-44 or SR-22 requirement, your policy needs to meet specific elevated or standard-minimum coverage limits, and your insurer files a certificate confirming this with the state. Otherwise, your policy structure looks the same as any standard Florida policy — the same coverage types, just with specific required limits and an added filing requirement.

## Your deductible, explained

A deductible is what you pay out of pocket before your comprehensive or collision coverage kicks in. A higher deductible generally means a lower premium, since you're taking on more of the initial risk yourself. This is a lever worth discussing with your agent based on your specific financial situation.

## Reading your declarations page

The declarations page — often just called the "dec page" — is the summary sheet at the front of your policy listing your specific coverage types, limits, deductibles, and premium. It's the fastest way to check exactly what you're covered for without wading through the full policy document. If you've never looked at yours closely, it's worth a few minutes to confirm everything matches what you actually agreed to and what your specific requirements, if any, call for.

## Policy components at a glance

| Component | Required or optional | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| PIP | Required | Your own medical expenses, regardless of fault |
| PDL | Required | Damage you cause to others' property |
| Bodily injury liability | Required in certain situations | Injuries you cause to others |
| Comprehensive | Optional (often lender-required) | Non-collision damage to your vehicle |
| Collision | Optional (often lender-required) | Collision damage to your vehicle |
| Uninsured motorist | Optional | Protection if hit by an uninsured driver |

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need every type of coverage listed here?** No — PIP and PDL are required for everyone; the rest depends on your specific situation, lender requirements, and personal risk tolerance.

**What's the difference between comprehensive and collision?** Comprehensive covers non-collision events like theft or weather damage; collision covers accident damage regardless of fault.

**Why does my policy cost more than a friend's with the same car?** Coverage limits, driving record, location, and carrier all factor in differently for each driver — even with an identical vehicle.

**Can I adjust my coverage after my policy starts?** Yes, in most cases, though changes may affect your premium and should be discussed with your agent.

**How do I know if I have enough coverage?** A good agent can walk through your specific situation and help you weigh required minimums against your actual financial exposure.
