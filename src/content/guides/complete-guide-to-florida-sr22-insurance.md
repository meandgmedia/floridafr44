If FR-44 has a quieter, more common sibling, it's SR-22. Far more Florida drivers deal with SR-22 requirements than most people realize, and yet the term still causes confusion the moment it shows up on a court notice. This guide brings everything together in one place.

## What SR-22 actually is

An SR-22 is a certificate — not a policy, not a special type of insurance product — that your insurance company files electronically with the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles. It confirms that you carry at least Florida's standard minimum liability coverage. The certificate attaches to a regular auto policy; it doesn't replace one.

## Who typically needs an SR-22 in Florida

- Drivers cited for driving without insurance
- Drivers with certain repeat moving violations
- Drivers reinstating a license after specific types of suspension
- Drivers specifically ordered by a Florida court to prove financial responsibility

If your situation instead involves a DUI or DWI conviction, you're more likely dealing with FR-44, which requires higher coverage limits. Our [FR-44 vs. SR-22 comparison](/guides/fr44-vs-sr22/) breaks down exactly how to tell the two apart.

## How long you'll need to carry it

Most SR-22 requirements in Florida run around three years, and that period must stay continuous. Any lapse — even briefly — gets reported to the state and can restart your filing period from zero. See our detailed breakdown of [what happens if you never file](/blog/what-happens-never-file-fr44-sr22-florida/) or let a [policy lapse](/blog/car-insurance-after-lapse-florida/) for the full picture of why continuity matters this much.

## What it costs

The certificate filing itself is typically free or low-cost. What actually drives your premium is the underlying violation and your broader driving record — not the SR-22 designation itself. Since SR-22 uses Florida's standard coverage limits rather than FR-44's elevated ones, it's generally less expensive to carry. Our full [SR-22 cost breakdown](/blog/sr22-insurance-cost-florida/) covers this in more detail, along with real strategies in our [cheap SR-22 insurance guide](/blog/cheap-sr22-insurance-florida/).

## The filing process, step by step

1. Confirm SR-22 (not FR-44) applies to your case
2. Gather your license, vehicle information, and any prior insurance details
3. Compare quotes from carriers that offer SR-22 filings — see our list of [carriers that offer SR-22 filings](/blog/insurance-companies-that-offer-sr22-florida/)
4. Activate your policy
5. Confirm your insurer files the certificate electronically
6. Maintain continuous coverage for your full required period

## Why the certificate itself rarely causes problems

Most of the friction people run into with SR-22 doesn't come from the filing itself — it comes from applying to the wrong carrier, misunderstanding which certificate applies to their case, or letting coverage lapse during a transition. If you've been [denied coverage](/blog/denied-insurance-no-prior-coverage-florida/) or run into confusion partway through the process, that's almost always a fixable mismatch rather than a sign coverage isn't available to you.

## How SR-22 pricing actually works

Because SR-22 doesn't require elevated coverage limits, the underlying violation and your broader driving record carry most of the weight in your final premium. ## Frequently asked questions

**Is SR-22 the same everywhere in the U.S.?** Most states use an SR-22 or similar equivalent, though specifics vary by state — Florida's process applies specifically to Florida-licensed drivers.

**Does SR-22 cost extra by itself?** The certificate filing is typically free or low-cost; your premium reflects the underlying violation and your driving record, not the filing itself.

**Can I switch carriers while under an SR-22 requirement?** Yes, as long as your new policy and filing start before your old one ends, avoiding any lapse.

**What happens once my SR-22 period ends?** Assuming no lapses, the requirement generally falls away and you can shop for standard coverage without the filing attached.
