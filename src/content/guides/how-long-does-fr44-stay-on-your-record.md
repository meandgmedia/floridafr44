There are actually two separate timelines to understand here: how long you must carry FR44 insurance, and how long the underlying conviction stays on your driving record. They're related but not identical.

## The FR44 filing period

Most Florida drivers must maintain FR44 coverage for three years without any lapse. This period is tracked by your insurance carrier's filings with the FLHSMV, and it must be continuous — a gap at any point can restart the clock.

## The conviction on your driving record

A DUI conviction itself typically stays on your Florida driving record for 75 years, and permanently on many background checks. This is different from the FR44 requirement, which is a temporary insurance filing tied to a specific period, not a permanent mark that insurers must always account for.

## How insurers view older convictions

While a DUI stays on your record long-term, its impact on your insurance premium generally lessens over time — particularly once the FR44 requirement itself ends and you're back on a standard policy. Some carriers weigh convictions from many years ago much less heavily than a recent one, though this varies by insurer.

## What happens right when your FR44 period ends

Once your required three-year (or court-specified) period is complete without a lapse, the FR44 requirement generally falls away. You can shop for a standard auto policy without the elevated coverage limits, and many drivers see their premium improve at this point.

## Does a completed FR44 period ever come back?

Generally, no — once satisfied, the FR44 requirement doesn't resurface unless a new qualifying violation occurs. That said, insurers can still ask about your driving history when quoting future policies, so it's worth understanding that the conviction itself remains part of your record even after the FR44 filing requirement ends.

## Staying ahead of the timeline

Confirm your specific FR44 start and end dates directly with your insurer or the FLHSMV — timelines can vary by case, and knowing your exact date helps you plan for when to shop for standard coverage again.
