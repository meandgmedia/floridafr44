FR44 insurance confuses a lot of Florida drivers, mostly because it's unique to just two states and rarely explained clearly. This guide covers everything in one place.

## What FR44 insurance actually is

FR44 is a certificate of financial responsibility. It is not a separate insurance product — it's a filing your insurance carrier submits electronically to the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV), confirming that your auto policy carries liability limits above Florida's standard minimums.

Florida requires FR44 (instead of the more commonly known SR22) primarily after DUI and DWI convictions, which is why the requirement feels unfamiliar even to drivers who've had other insurance experience.

## Who typically needs an FR44

- Drivers convicted of DUI or DWI
- Drivers with certain repeat serious traffic offenses
- Drivers reinstating a license after a DUI-related suspension
- Drivers specifically notified by a Florida court or the FLHSMV

If you've received an official notice mentioning "FR44," that document is the definitive source for your specific requirement — including your exact filing period and any court-ordered conditions.

## Required coverage limits

FR44 requires higher liability limits than Florida's standard minimum policy. Because required limits can be adjusted by the state, always confirm current minimums with your insurer or the FLHSMV rather than relying on limits from a prior year.

## How long the requirement lasts

Most FR44 filings run for three years, and that period must be continuous. Any lapse in coverage — even a single day — must be reported by your insurer and can restart the clock, extending your total requirement well beyond three years.

## What it costs

FR44 policies typically cost more than standard auto insurance for two reasons: higher required coverage limits, and the increased risk associated with the underlying conviction. That said, pricing varies substantially by carrier. Getting quotes from multiple insurers is consistently the most effective way to find a competitive rate — see our full breakdown in [how much FR44 insurance costs](/blog/how-much-does-fr44-insurance-cost/).

## The filing process, step by step

1. Confirm your requirement and specific dates from your court or FLHSMV notice
2. Get quotes from carriers that offer FR44 filings
3. Select a policy and complete payment to activate it
4. Your insurer files the FR44 certificate electronically with the state
5. Once received, the filing supports license reinstatement (if applicable)
6. Maintain continuous coverage for your full required period

## How to lower your cost

- Compare multiple carriers — pricing varies significantly for FR44 risk specifically
- Keep coverage continuous to avoid restarting your filing period
- Ask about every applicable discount, including defensive driving courses and paid-in-full options
- Maintain a clean record for the remainder of your filing period

## Common misconceptions

**"FR44 is its own insurance policy."** It's an add-on filing to a standard auto policy, not a separate product.

**"Any insurance company can file it."** Not true — only some carriers offer FR44 filings, so confirming this before committing to a quote matters.

**"FR44 and SR22 are interchangeable."** They serve a similar purpose, but FR44 requires higher limits and is tied to different violations. See our [FR44 vs. SR22 comparison](/guides/fr44-vs-sr22/) for the full picture.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I switch carriers while under an FR44 requirement?** Yes, as long as the new policy and filing begin before the old one ends, to avoid any lapse.

**Does my rate improve after the FR44 period ends?** Often, yes — both because required limits typically decrease and because the conviction becomes more distant.

**What if I move to a different Florida city?** Update your address with your carrier promptly; your rate and filing details are tied to your policy information.
