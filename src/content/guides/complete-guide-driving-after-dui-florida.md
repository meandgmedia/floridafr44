A DUI conviction sets off a chain of requirements that can feel overwhelming to untangle all at once — license suspension, court conditions, insurance requirements, reinstatement fees. This guide brings the whole picture together so you know exactly what's ahead and in what order.

## Step one: understand what actually happened to your license

Most DUI convictions in Florida trigger a license suspension. The length varies based on whether it's a first offense or a repeat conviction, and whether other factors — like a very high BAC or an accident — were involved. Your court paperwork specifies your exact suspension terms; this is your definitive source, not general assumptions based on someone else's case.

## Step two: understand the FR-44 requirement

A DUI conviction in Florida typically triggers an FR-44 requirement — a certificate your insurer files electronically confirming you carry liability coverage above the state's standard minimums, generally for three years without any lapse. Our [complete guide to Florida FR-44 insurance](/guides/complete-guide-to-florida-fr44-insurance/) covers this in full depth, and [DUI auto insurance in Florida](/blog/dui-insurance-florida/) explains specifically how a DUI reshapes your insurance situation.

## Step three: address any court-ordered conditions

Beyond insurance, many DUI cases involve additional requirements — a DUI education program, community service, probation terms, or an ignition interlock device requirement. These vary by case and are set by the court, not by your insurance company. Completing these promptly keeps your overall timeline moving.

## Step four: shop for FR-44-compliant coverage

Not every insurer writes FR-44 policies, so this step benefits enormously from working with someone who already knows which carriers do. Compare multiple quotes — [pricing varies significantly between carriers](/blog/how-fr44-insurance-works-in-florida/) for FR-44 risk specifically, more than for almost any other coverage type.

## Step five: pay any reinstatement fees

Florida charges a reinstatement fee tied to your specific suspension type. This is separate from your insurance costs and is paid directly to the FLHSMV once your other requirements are met.

## Step six: confirm your license status

Once your FR-44 policy is active, your insurer files the certificate, and your other court-ordered conditions are complete, you can confirm your reinstated status through the FLHSMV. Our guide on [how to reinstate your Florida driver license](/guides/how-to-reinstate-your-florida-driver-license/) walks through this final stage in detail.

## What if this isn't your first DUI?

Repeat convictions typically mean longer suspension periods, stricter conditions, and — from an insurance standpoint — a narrower pool of carriers willing to write your policy. Our guides on [insurance after a second DUI](/blog/second-dui-insurance-florida/) and [auto insurance after multiple DUIs](/blog/multiple-dui-insurance-florida/) address these situations specifically and honestly.

## What if you don't currently own a car?

The FR-44 requirement still applies. Non-owner FR-44 policies exist for exactly this situation — see our [complete guide to non-owner insurance](/guides/complete-guide-to-non-owner-insurance-florida/) for the full picture.

## The emotional side of this process

It's worth naming directly: this process is stressful, and that stress is a normal response to a genuinely difficult situation, not a sign you're handling it wrong. Breaking the overall timeline into these six discrete steps tends to help, since it turns an overwhelming, vague sense of "I have so much to deal with" into a concrete checklist you can work through one item at a time. Most people find that once insurance — often the piece that feels most confusing — is actually handled, the rest of the process feels considerably more manageable.

## Frequently asked questions

**How soon after a DUI can I start shopping for insurance?** Immediately — there's no need to wait for your court case to fully resolve before comparing FR-44 quotes.

**Does every DUI require FR-44?** Most do — our guide on [whether every DUI requires FR-44](/blog/does-florida-require-fr44-after-dui/) covers the exceptions and how to confirm your specific case.

**Will my insurance rate ever return to normal?** Often, yes, especially once your FR-44 period ends and time passes since the conviction — though the conviction itself remains on your record long-term.

**What's the single biggest mistake people make after a DUI?** Letting FR-44 coverage lapse due to a missed payment, which can restart the entire three-year requirement.
