If you're trying to figure out why one friend pays more for their state-required coverage than another, the answer often comes down to which certificate they're carrying. Here's a direct, practical comparison of FR-44 and SR-22 costs and coverage in Florida.

## The core coverage difference

This is the root of the cost gap: FR-44 requires liability coverage above Florida's standard minimums, while SR-22 uses the state's standard minimum limits. More required coverage generally means a higher premium, independent of anything else about the driver.

## Why FR-44 typically costs more overall

Beyond the higher coverage limits, FR-44 is specifically tied to DUI and DWI convictions, which insurers weigh as higher risk in their own right. SR-22 covers a broader range of situations — some higher risk, some more moderate — which means its cost impact varies more depending on the specific underlying violation.

## Side-by-side cost and coverage comparison

| Factor | SR-22 | FR-44 |
|---|---|---|
| Required liability limits | Florida's standard minimums | Above standard minimums |
| Typical trigger | Uninsured driving, certain violations, reinstatement | DUI/DWI conviction |
| Relative cost impact | Moderate | Larger, due to higher limits plus risk |
| Filing period | Typically ~3 years | Typically ~3 years, no lapses |
| States that use this specific form | Most states use an equivalent | Only Florida and Virginia |

## Why comparing carriers matters more for FR-44

Because FR-44 combines higher required limits with DUI-related risk pricing, the gap between the cheapest and most expensive quote for the same driver tends to be larger than with SR-22. This makes comparison shopping even more valuable for FR-44 drivers specifically.

## What doesn't differ between the two

Both are certificates, not standalone insurance products — both attach to a regular auto policy. Both are filed electronically by your insurer. Both require continuous coverage for their full filing period, and a lapse in either one carries similar consequences: potential suspension and a restarted filing period.

## A practical example of how the gap plays out

Picture two drivers with similar records: one needs SR-22 after a lapse in coverage, the other needs FR-44 after a DUI. Even before factoring in any difference in their driving history, the FR-44 driver starts from a higher baseline simply because of the elevated coverage limits required. Add in how insurers price DUI-related risk on top of that, and the total gap between what each driver pays can be substantial — which is exactly why treating these as two different shopping exercises, rather than assuming similar pricing, matters.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is FR-44 always more expensive than SR-22?** Generally, yes, due to the combination of higher required limits and DUI-related risk pricing, though individual circumstances vary.

**Can my requirement change from SR-22 to FR-44, or vice versa?** Only if the underlying legal situation changes — your court or FLHSMV notice reflects your current, specific requirement.

**Does comparing carriers help more with one than the other?** Comparison shopping helps with both, but the potential savings are often larger with FR-44 given the wider pricing spread between carriers.

**Will my FR-44 or SR-22 cost ever match a standard policy?** Once your required filing period ends without a lapse, you can typically shop for standard coverage, often at meaningfully better rates than during your filing period.

**Which one do I actually need?** Your official court or FLHSMV notice is the definitive source — if it's unclear, we can help you confirm it.
