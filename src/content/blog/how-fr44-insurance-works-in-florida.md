You've heard the term. Maybe a court clerk said it, maybe a letter from the FLHSMV did. Either way, "FR-44" is now a word you need to understand, and fast. Here's how the whole mechanism actually works, start to finish.

## It starts with a notice, not an application

Nobody wakes up and decides to get FR-44 insurance. It's assigned to you — typically by a Florida court following a DUI or DWI conviction, or by the FLHSMV as part of reinstating a suspended license. That notice is your source of truth: it tells you that FR-44 (not the more commonly known SR-22) applies to your case, and it usually spells out your specific filing period.

## The mechanics behind the form

FR-44 is a certificate, not a policy. Your insurance company files it electronically with the state, confirming that your auto policy carries liability limits above Florida's standard minimums. You're not buying "FR-44 insurance" as a distinct product — you're buying a regular auto policy built to satisfy a higher bar, with the FR-44 filing attached on top.

## Step by step, here's the full process

1. **You confirm the requirement.** Your court or FLHSMV notice tells you it's FR-44, along with your specific dates.
2. **You shop carriers that actually write FR-44 policies.** Not every insurer does, so this step alone saves a lot of wasted time.
3. **You select a policy and pay to activate it.** This locks in your coverage at the required limits.
4. **Your insurer files the FR-44 electronically.** This usually happens automatically — you don't submit anything yourself.
5. **The state receives the filing.** This is typically the piece that clears the way for license reinstatement, alongside any other court-ordered conditions.
6. **You maintain the policy without any lapse.** For the length of your required period — usually three years.

## Why the details matter more than they seem to

The part people underestimate is step six. A single lapse — even for a day — gets reported by your insurer to the state, which can suspend your license again and, worse, restart your entire filing period. If you take away only one thing from this article, let it be that: keep the policy active, no exceptions.

## FR-44 process at a glance

| Stage | What's happening | Who's responsible |
|---|---|---|
| Notification | Court/FLHSMV informs you of the requirement | State/court |
| Shopping | Comparing carriers that write FR-44 | You (or your agent) |
| Activation | Policy starts, payment confirmed | You + carrier |
| Filing | FR-44 certificate submitted electronically | Your insurer |
| Compliance period | ~3 years, continuous coverage required | You |

## Common mistakes people make at each stage

Even a straightforward process trips people up in predictable ways. Assuming any insurer can file FR-44 is probably the most common one — plenty of drivers get excited about a cheap general auto quote, only to find out the carrier doesn't actually support FR-44 filings. Another frequent stumble: confusing FR-44 with SR-22 and shopping for the wrong coverage limits entirely. And then there's the quiet one — letting an autopay fail or a card expire without noticing, which triggers exactly the lapse this whole process is designed to avoid.

None of these mistakes are catastrophic if you catch them early. They're mostly avoidable with a little organization and a quick double-check of your paperwork before you commit to a policy.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to do the filing myself?** No — once your policy is active, your insurance company submits the FR-44 certificate directly to the FLHSMV.

**Can I choose any insurance company?** Only companies that write FR-44 policies. Confirming this upfront saves a lot of frustration.

**What if I already have car insurance?** If your current carrier offers FR-44 filings and can adjust your limits, they may be able to add it to your existing policy. If not, you'll need a new one.

**Is there a way to speed up the process?** Having your court notice, license, and vehicle info ready before you start shopping is the biggest time-saver.

**What happens after my three years are up?** Assuming no lapses, the FR-44 requirement generally falls away and you can shop for a standard policy again — often at a noticeably better rate than you started with.
