If you've been told to "go get an FR-44," that's a fairly vague instruction for something with real deadlines attached. So let's make it concrete: here's exactly how to get one, in order, without wasting a week figuring out where to start.

## First, confirm what you actually need

Before anything else, find your court paperwork or FLHSMV notice. It should say "FR-44" explicitly. If it says "SR-22" instead, you're on a different (and generally less expensive) path — check our [FR-44 vs. SR-22 guide](/guides/fr44-vs-sr22/) if you're not sure which applies.

## Gather what you'll need

- Driver's license
- Vehicle details (year, make, model, VIN if handy)
- Your court or FLHSMV notice, with your specific requirement and dates
- Any prior insurance information, if you're switching carriers

## Compare more than one quote

FR-44 pricing swings a lot between insurers for the exact same driver. Getting two or three quotes instead of one is consistently the biggest lever for keeping your cost down — see our guide on [cheap FR-44 insurance in Florida](/blog/cheap-fr44-insurance-florida/) for more.

## Activate your policy

Once you pick a carrier, you'll set up the policy with the required liability limits and get it active. This is usually the step where payment is required — most carriers won't file FR-44 until the policy is live.

## Let your insurer file the certificate

Here's the good news: you don't file anything yourself. Once your policy is active, your carrier submits the FR-44 electronically to the FLHSMV. This typically happens automatically, without you needing to follow up.

## Keep it active, no exceptions

The finish line isn't getting the FR-44 filed — it's keeping your policy continuously active for your full required period, usually three years. A lapse anywhere in that window can restart the clock.

## What to do if something goes wrong mid-process

Sometimes a hiccup happens — a carrier you expected to write FR-44 turns out not to, or paperwork arrives slower than expected. If that happens, don't panic and don't just wait it out. Reach back out to whoever's helping you shop (or start a new quote request) and explain exactly where things stand. Most delays are fixable quickly once someone actually looks at the specifics, and waiting silently usually costs more time than asking for help.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does the whole process take?** Often just a day or two once you have your paperwork ready — sometimes faster.

**Do I need a lawyer to get FR-44 insurance?** No. FR-44 is an insurance matter, not a legal filing you need representation for, though your attorney may be involved in the broader DUI case.

**Can I get FR-44 without owning a car?** Yes — non-owner FR-44 policies exist for drivers in exactly this situation.

**What if I already have a lapse on my record?** It doesn't disqualify you from getting FR-44 coverage now, though it's worth discussing with your agent how it might affect pricing.

**What if my court date and my insurance deadline don't line up?** Reach out to your agent as soon as you notice the mismatch — timing issues are common and usually solvable with enough notice, but they get harder to fix the closer you get to a deadline.
