Reckless driving is treated more seriously than a typical moving violation, both by Florida law and by insurance companies. If you're dealing with this citation, here's what actually happens next.

## Why reckless driving hits harder than a standard ticket

Unlike routine violations like speeding, reckless driving in Florida is a criminal offense, not just a civil infraction. Insurers treat it accordingly — it signals a meaningfully different risk profile than a simple ticket, and pricing reflects that difference.

## What changes with your policy

Expect a significant premium increase, and depending on the specifics of your case, you may face non-renewal from your current carrier. Some insurers decline to write policies for reckless driving convictions entirely, which narrows your options to carriers that specifically work with this type of risk.

## Does reckless driving require FR-44 or SR-22?

Not automatically, but depending on the circumstances — particularly if alcohol or drugs were involved, or if the court imposes specific conditions — a filing requirement can apply. Check your court paperwork carefully, since reckless driving cases vary more than standard tickets in what they ultimately require.

## How long the impact typically lasts

Reckless driving convictions tend to affect your insurance rate longer than a standard ticket, often for several years rather than one renewal cycle. The exact duration varies by carrier, but patience and a clean record afterward remain the most reliable path back to standard pricing.

## Reckless driving insurance impact

| Factor | Typical outcome |
|---|---|
| Premium impact | Significant increase, more than standard tickets |
| Carrier availability | Narrower — some insurers decline this risk entirely |
| Possible filing requirement | Depends on case specifics — check court paperwork |
| Duration of impact | Often longer than standard violations |

## What to do right after the citation

1. Understand the full legal implications, which may require consulting an attorney separately from your insurance situation
2. Check whether your case involves any specific insurance filing requirement
3. Start comparing carriers that work with reckless driving convictions
4. Avoid any further violations, which would compound the situation significantly
5. Maintain continuous coverage throughout the process

## Why this citation sometimes surprises people

Reckless driving can sometimes result from behavior that felt relatively minor in the moment — a specific speed threshold, a particular maneuver — but carries more serious legal and insurance consequences than the driver initially expected. If this describes your situation, know that the insurance path forward is still manageable, even though the citation itself carries more weight than a typical ticket.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will my current insurer definitely drop me?** Not always, but it's common enough that shopping around proactively is worth doing regardless.

**Does reckless driving always require FR-44 or SR-22?** Not automatically — it depends on the specific circumstances of your case, so check your court paperwork carefully.

**How much will my rate increase?** It varies significantly by carrier, which is exactly why comparing multiple quotes matters more here than with routine violations.

**Can I get standard coverage again eventually?** Yes, typically once enough time passes with a clean record, though the timeline is often longer than for standard tickets.

**Should I contest the citation before shopping for insurance?** That's a legal question worth discussing with an attorney — from an insurance standpoint, it's still worth understanding your coverage options in parallel.
