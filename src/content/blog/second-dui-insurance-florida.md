A second DUI conviction lands differently than a first — heavier penalties, more scrutiny, and yes, a bigger insurance impact. Here's an honest look at what changes and what your realistic options are.

## How a second offense changes your requirement

You'll still need an FR-44 filing, same as after a first DUI, but the surrounding conditions are often stricter. Suspension periods can be longer, courts may impose additional requirements, and insurers generally weigh a second conviction more heavily than a first when calculating your rate.

## Why insurers treat repeat convictions differently

A single DUI can sometimes be viewed as an isolated event. A second one signals a pattern, at least from a risk-assessment standpoint, and pricing tends to reflect that. This doesn't mean affordable coverage is out of reach — it means comparing carriers matters even more than it did the first time around.

## Not every carrier will work with you — and that's fine

Some insurers simply don't write policies for drivers with multiple DUI convictions. That's not a reflection of your situation being hopeless; it just means the pool of eligible carriers is smaller, which makes working with someone who already knows which companies say yes even more valuable.

## What stays consistent

Your core coverage requirements — PIP, Property Damage Liability, and the higher FR-44 liability limits — work the same way regardless of whether it's your first or second conviction. The filing process itself doesn't change; what changes is pricing and carrier availability.

## Second DUI considerations

| Factor | First DUI | Second DUI |
|---|---|---|
| FR-44 requirement | Yes | Yes |
| Typical filing period | ~3 years | ~3 years, sometimes longer conditions |
| Carrier availability | Broader | Narrower — fewer insurers accept repeat offenses |
| Rate impact | Significant | Generally higher |
| Court conditions | Standard | Often stricter |

## A note on patience

If you're feeling like doors are closing, take a breath. Specialized agencies work with repeat-offense drivers regularly, and coverage is available — it just takes someone who knows exactly which carriers to approach instead of getting declined by five general insurers first.

## What if you're also dealing with a license suspension?

A second DUI often comes with a longer suspension period than a first. It's worth tackling the insurance piece early even if reinstatement is still weeks or months away — having your FR-44 policy already active means one less thing to arrange when the rest of your requirements are finally met, and it avoids any last-minute scramble.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I still get FR-44 coverage after a second DUI?** Yes, though your carrier options will be narrower than after a first offense.

**Will my rate ever improve?** Often gradually, especially with a clean record following the conviction, though the improvement curve tends to be slower than after a first offense.

**Does a second DUI extend my FR-44 filing period?** Sometimes, depending on your specific court order — always confirm your exact dates with your notice.

**Should I expect to be declined by some carriers?** Possibly, yes — this is normal for repeat-offense cases and not a sign you won't find coverage elsewhere.

**What's the best first step after a second DUI?** Get your court paperwork in order and start comparing carriers that specifically work with repeat-offense drivers, rather than shopping generally and hoping for the best.
