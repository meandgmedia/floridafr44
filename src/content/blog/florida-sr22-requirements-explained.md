SR-22 requirements sound bureaucratic, but they boil down to a short list of things you need to get right. Here's the complete picture, without the legal jargon.

## The coverage requirement

SR-22 requires you to carry at least Florida's standard minimum liability coverage — Personal Injury Protection and Property Damage Liability, generally alongside bodily injury liability once an SR-22 is involved. Unlike FR-44, SR-22 doesn't require elevated limits above the standard minimums.

## Who Florida typically requires this from

- Drivers cited for driving without insurance
- Drivers with certain repeat moving violations
- Drivers reinstating a license after specific suspensions
- Anyone specifically ordered by a Florida court to prove financial responsibility

If your situation involves a DUI or DWI conviction, you're more likely looking at FR-44 instead — check our [FR-44 vs. SR-22 breakdown](/guides/fr44-vs-sr22/) to confirm which applies to you.

## The filing period requirement

Most SR-22 requirements run around three years in Florida, and — same as FR-44 — that period needs to stay continuous. Any lapse gets reported to the state and can restart your clock.

## The carrier requirement

Not every Florida insurer files SR-22 certificates. Confirm this upfront with any carrier you're considering, rather than assuming a cheap quote automatically includes the filing.

## Why these requirements exist in the first place

It's easy to see SR-22 purely as red tape, but the underlying purpose is fairly practical: Florida wants extra assurance that a driver who's already shown a gap in coverage — whether through an uninsured citation, a suspension, or a court order — is actually carrying what the law requires going forward. The certificate itself doesn't punish you further; it just documents that you're compliant, in a way the state can verify without taking your word for it. Understanding that context sometimes makes the whole process feel less like an arbitrary hoop and more like a temporary, reasonable check.

## SR-22 requirements at a glance

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Coverage limits | Florida's standard minimums |
| Filing period | Typically ~3 years, continuous |
| Filing method | Electronic, submitted by your insurer |
| Carrier eligibility | Must offer SR-22 filings |
| Consequence of lapse | Suspension risk + possible restart of period |

## A closer look at continuous coverage

"Continuous" sounds simple until you're the one juggling due dates. In practice, it means your policy can never show a gap — not for a missed payment, not for a canceled card, not even for a few days while you switch carriers without careful timing. If you're planning to switch insurers during your SR-22 period, always confirm the new policy and filing start before the old one ends. It's a small planning step that prevents a genuinely costly mistake.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can my SR-22 requirement change mid-filing?** It's uncommon, but always confirm your current requirement rather than assuming.

**Do all Florida courts require the same SR-22 terms?** Terms are generally similar, but always defer to your specific notice for exact dates and conditions.

**What if I already have a policy — do I need a new one?** Not necessarily. If your current carrier files SR-22 certificates, they may be able to add it to your existing policy.

**Is SR-22 required forever?** No — once your filing period is complete without a lapse, the requirement generally ends.

**What's the very first thing I should check on my notice?** Confirm it actually says "SR-22" and not "FR-44" — the two get mixed up constantly, and they carry different coverage requirements.
