One of the most common questions we hear is simple: how long does this actually last? Here's what most Florida drivers can expect.

## The typical filing period

Most FR44 requirements in Florida last three years, and that period must be continuous — meaning no gaps or lapses in coverage. Your specific requirement is set by the court or the FLHSMV, so it's worth confirming the exact length against the notice you received.

## Why continuity matters so much

If your policy lapses at any point during the filing period, your insurer is required to report it to the state. That can lead to a renewed license suspension and, in many cases, restarts the clock on your required filing period. In other words, a single missed payment can turn a three-year requirement into a much longer one.

## What happens when the period ends

Once your required filing period is complete, the FR44 requirement generally falls away, and you can shop for a standard auto policy without the higher mandated limits. Many drivers see their rate improve at this point — both because the higher coverage requirement ends and because the conviction becomes more distant.

## Staying on track

The best way to protect your timeline is straightforward: pay on time, keep your policy active without interruption, and avoid additional violations that could extend your requirement. If you're not sure exactly where you stand, your insurer or the FLHSMV can confirm your specific dates.
