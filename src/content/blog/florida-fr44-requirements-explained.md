Requirements aren't exactly thrilling reading, but with FR-44, the details are the whole ballgame. Miss one, and you could be looking at a longer suspension or a restarted filing period. Let's go through exactly what Florida expects of you.

## The core requirement: higher liability limits

The defining feature of FR-44 is coverage above Florida's standard minimums. Exact required limits can be adjusted by the state over time, so rather than quote a number that might be outdated by the time you read this, the reliable move is confirming current limits directly with your insurer or the FLHSMV — they'll match your policy to whatever's currently required.

## Who this requirement applies to

FR-44 is specifically tied to:

- DUI and DWI convictions
- Certain repeat serious violations
- License reinstatement following a DUI-related suspension

If your situation doesn't involve a DUI-type conviction, you may be looking at SR-22 instead, which uses standard coverage limits. Our [FR-44 vs. SR-22 guide](/guides/fr44-vs-sr22/) walks through exactly how to tell which applies to you.

## The filing period requirement

Most FR-44 requirements run three years, and — this is the part that trips people up — that period has to be continuous. No lapses, no gaps, no "I forgot to pay for a week." A single interruption gets reported to the state and can restart your clock from zero.

## The carrier requirement

Not every insurance company in Florida offers FR-44 filings. This isn't optional or negotiable on your end — if a carrier doesn't support FR-44, they simply can't file it for you, no matter how good their regular auto rates look.

## Requirements summary table

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Liability limits | Above Florida's standard minimums (confirm current figures with your insurer) |
| Filing period | Typically 3 years, continuous |
| Filing method | Electronic, submitted by your carrier |
| Carrier eligibility | Must be an FR-44-approved insurer |
| Lapse consequence | Suspension risk + possible restart of filing period |

## What happens if you don't meet a requirement

Missing any piece of this — wrong coverage limits, a lapse, or an ineligible carrier — generally means your filing doesn't satisfy the court or FLHSMV's order. That can delay license reinstatement or trigger a fresh suspension, even if the miss was accidental.

## The most common way requirements get misunderstood

A lot of confusion starts with assumptions. Someone hears "FR-44" from a friend who actually needed SR-22, and suddenly they're shopping for the wrong coverage entirely. Or they assume their filing period matches what a cousin's case required, when the actual dates on their own notice say something different. The fix for all of this is the same: always go back to your own official paperwork rather than someone else's experience, since even small details can differ case by case.

It's also worth knowing that Florida's specific coverage limits for FR-44 can be updated by the state over time. A number you saw referenced a few years ago might not match what's currently required, which is exactly why confirming directly with your insurer — rather than relying on an old article or a friend's outdated policy — matters so much here.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can my required limits change during my filing period?** It's uncommon, but always confirm your current requirement rather than assuming it matches what you were told initially or what a friend's case required.

**Does FR-44 apply the same way to every DUI case?** Generally yes, though repeat offenses or additional violations can sometimes carry stricter conditions from the court.

**What if I move to a different Florida city mid-filing?** Update your address with your insurer promptly — your filing details are tied to your policy information.

**Can I request an early end to my FR-44 period?** Typically no. The filing period is set by the court or FLHSMV and must be completed as ordered.

**What if I'm not sure whether my notice says FR-44 or SR-22?** Read it closely, and if it's still unclear, reach out directly — a quick text with a photo of your notice is usually enough to confirm which requirement actually applies to you.
