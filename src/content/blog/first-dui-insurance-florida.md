A first DUI is disorienting in a way that's hard to explain until you're living it. Everything about your normal routine — driving to work, picking up groceries, just existing — suddenly runs through a new set of rules. Here's what actually changes with your insurance, without the legal jargon.

## The immediate requirement: FR-44

After a first DUI conviction in Florida, you'll typically need an FR-44 filing to keep driving legally. This is a certificate your insurance company files electronically with the state, confirming you carry coverage above Florida's standard minimums, generally for three years.

## Why "first" doesn't mean "minor" to insurers

It's tempting to assume a first offense gets treated gently, but from an insurance pricing standpoint, the conviction itself matters more than whether it's your first or third. That said, a clean record before the conviction and a clean one afterward both work in your favor over time — first-time offenders with otherwise strong driving histories often see their rates improve faster than repeat offenders.

## What to expect, step by step

1. Your current insurer may non-renew or raise your rate significantly
2. You'll need to find a carrier that offers FR-44 filings
3. You'll compare quotes — and pricing can vary a lot between insurers
4. Your new policy activates, and your carrier files the FR-44 electronically
5. You maintain the policy without any lapse for your full required period

## The good news about first offenses

Assuming there's no repeat violation, a first DUI's insurance impact tends to soften noticeably once your required filing period ends. Many drivers see their rate improve meaningfully in year two and three of their FR-44 period as the conviction becomes more distant, and even more once the FR-44 requirement itself concludes.

## First DUI insurance timeline

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| Conviction | FR-44 requirement typically begins |
| Weeks 1–2 | Shop and activate new FR-44-compliant policy |
| Year 1 | Highest rate impact |
| Years 2–3 | Often gradual improvement with clean record |
| After FR-44 ends | Standard policy shopping resumes |

## Talking to family or an employer about the change

A jump in your insurance premium sometimes needs an explanation, whether it's a spouse noticing the household budget shift or an employer that requires proof of insurance for a work vehicle. Being upfront and specific — "my policy now includes a required FR-44 filing following a conviction, and it runs for three years" — tends to go over better than a vague explanation, and it shows you understand exactly where you stand.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a first DUI always require FR-44?** Most do — your court or FLHSMV notice will confirm your specific requirement.

**Will my rate ever return to what it was before?** Often it improves substantially, though the conviction itself remains part of your driving history long-term.

**Can I reduce my rate faster than three years?** Not the FR-44 requirement's length, but comparing carriers and maintaining a clean record both help your actual premium during that period.

**Is a first DUI treated differently from repeat offenses for insurance purposes?** Generally, yes — repeat convictions tend to carry a heavier and longer-lasting pricing impact than a single, isolated offense.

**What's the most important thing to get right after a first DUI?** Avoiding any lapse in coverage. It's the single biggest risk to both your license status and your long-term insurance cost.
