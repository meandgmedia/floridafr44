Insurance shorthand like "100/300/50" looks like a code you need a decoder ring for, but it's actually pretty simple once someone breaks it down. Here's what those numbers mean for FR-44 coverage in Florida.

## Breaking down the three numbers

These figures represent liability coverage limits, expressed in thousands of dollars:

- **First number (100):** Bodily injury liability per person — the maximum your policy pays for injuries to one person in an accident you cause
- **Second number (300):** Bodily injury liability per accident — the maximum total payout for injuries across everyone involved in a single accident
- **Third number (50):** Property damage liability — the maximum your policy pays for damage to someone else's property

## Why FR-44 requires higher limits than standard policies

These figures represent limits above Florida's standard minimum requirements, which is exactly what makes a policy FR-44-compliant rather than a standard policy. The higher limits reflect the state's requirement for extra financial responsibility following certain convictions, most commonly DUI-related.

## Important: confirm your current required limits

Because required limits can be adjusted by the state over time, don't rely on a specific number you saw referenced elsewhere without confirming it's still current. Your insurer or the FLHSMV can confirm the exact limits that apply to your policy right now.

## What these limits actually protect

If you cause an accident, these limits determine how much your insurance pays toward the other party's injuries and property damage before you'd be personally responsible for anything beyond those amounts. Higher limits mean more protection for the people you might affect in an accident — and for your own finances, since amounts beyond your coverage limits could come out of your own pocket.

## Coverage limit breakdown

| Limit component | What it covers | Typical FR-44 requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Bodily injury, per person | Injuries to one person | Above Florida's standard minimum |
| Bodily injury, per accident | Total injuries across an accident | Above Florida's standard minimum |
| Property damage | Damage to others' property | Above Florida's standard minimum |

## Why understanding your limits matters beyond compliance

Beyond simply satisfying the FR-44 requirement, understanding what these numbers actually protect helps you make an informed decision if you're considering coverage above the minimum. A serious accident can easily exceed lower liability limits, leaving you personally responsible for the difference — which is worth weighing even if your legal requirement technically stops at the FR-44 minimum.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these limits the same for every FR-44 policyholder?** Generally, the state sets specific required limits for FR-44 policies, though it's worth confirming your exact current requirement rather than assuming.

**Can I choose higher limits than required?** Yes — you can always opt for more coverage than the minimum FR-44 requirement, which some drivers choose for extra protection.

**Do these limits affect my premium?** Yes, higher limits generally mean a higher premium, since your insurer is taking on more potential financial exposure.

**What happens if my coverage falls below the required limits?** Your policy would no longer satisfy your FR-44 requirement, which can jeopardize your compliance status with the state.

**Where can I find my exact required limits?** Your court or FLHSMV notice, or your insurance agent, can confirm the specific limits that apply to your case.
