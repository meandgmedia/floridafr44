Not owning a car doesn't get you out of an FR-44 requirement — a lot of people are surprised to learn this the hard way. If you're in this exact situation, here's the good news: non-owner FR-44 insurance exists specifically for you, and it's more straightforward than it sounds.

## Why you'd need FR-44 without a car

FR-44 requirements are tied to your driving record and legal status, not to vehicle ownership. If your Florida court or FLHSMV notice specifies FR-44 following a DUI conviction, that requirement stands regardless of whether you currently own, lease, or borrow a car. Plenty of people in this position drive occasionally — a rental, a friend's car, a family member's vehicle — and still need proof of financial responsibility on file.

## How non-owner FR-44 policies work

A non-owner policy provides liability coverage that follows you as a driver rather than covering a specific vehicle. It satisfies your FR-44 requirement the same way a standard policy would, with your insurer filing the certificate electronically once your policy is active. The coverage limits still need to meet FR-44's elevated requirements above Florida's standard minimums.

## What it does and doesn't cover

Non-owner policies typically provide liability coverage — protecting you if you cause an accident while driving a vehicle you don't own. They generally don't include comprehensive or collision coverage, since there's no specific vehicle attached to the policy to protect. If you occasionally drive a specific car regularly, like a family member's vehicle, it's worth discussing with your agent whether a non-owner policy or being added to that vehicle's existing policy makes more sense.

## Who typically needs this

- Drivers who had their license suspended and don't currently own a vehicle
- Drivers who rely on rideshares, rentals, or borrowed cars
- Drivers between vehicles who still need to maintain continuous FR-44 coverage
- Drivers rebuilding their situation after a conviction, before they're ready to purchase a car again

## A practical note on timing

If you're getting a non-owner FR-44 policy specifically to reinstate a suspended license, timing matters the same way it would with any FR-44 policy — the coverage generally needs to be active before reinstatement is finalized. Getting this piece sorted early avoids it being the bottleneck holding up the rest of your paperwork.

## How rates typically compare to a standard policy

Since there's no vehicle attached to a non-owner policy, insurers generally see less exposure than with a standard FR-44 policy covering a specific car — no risk of vehicle damage claims, no need to factor in a car's value or repair costs. This usually translates to a lower premium than you'd pay insuring an actual vehicle at the same FR-44 liability limits, though your individual driving history still plays the biggest role in your exact rate. It's worth getting an actual quote rather than assuming a specific dollar amount, since the gap between carriers can be meaningful even within the non-owner category.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a non-owner policy cost less than a standard FR-44 policy?** Often, yes, since there's no specific vehicle to insure — but confirm with your agent, as your specific driving history still factors into pricing.

**Can I switch to a standard policy later if I buy a car?** Yes, and your existing FR-44 filing typically transfers to the new policy as long as coverage stays continuous.

**Does a non-owner policy cover a car I borrow regularly?** Generally yes for liability purposes, though check with your insurer about any restrictions on how often or which vehicles apply.

**What if I get FR-44 coverage as a non-owner and then buy a car?** Update your policy right away — you'll likely need to add the vehicle and may need additional coverage types like comprehensive or collision.

**Is non-owner FR-44 insurance harder to find than standard FR-44 policies?** Not necessarily, though not every FR-44-eligible carrier offers a non-owner option — confirming this upfront saves time.
