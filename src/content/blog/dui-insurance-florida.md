A DUI changes more than your driving record — it reshapes your entire insurance situation, often in ways nobody explains clearly until you're already in the middle of it. Here's the complete picture, in plain language.

## The insurance consequence that follows a DUI

In Florida, a DUI or DWI conviction typically triggers an FR-44 requirement. That's a certificate your insurance company files electronically with the state, confirming you carry liability coverage above Florida's standard minimums. It's not a separate insurance product — it's an add-on requirement layered onto a regular auto policy.

## Why this catches people off guard

Most states use a form called SR-22 for DUI-related cases. Florida (and Virginia) use FR-44 instead, which requires higher coverage limits than SR-22. If you've had a DUI in another state before and now live in Florida, or you're just hearing about this for the first time, the unfamiliar name alone can make an already stressful situation feel more complicated than it needs to be.

## How much your rate actually changes

There's no single number here — it depends on your driving history, your vehicle, where you live, and critically, which carrier you choose. FR-44 pricing varies more between insurers than almost any other policy type, since each company weighs DUI-related risk with its own formula. That's exactly why comparing multiple quotes matters more after a DUI than at almost any other point.

## What stays the same

You still need the same core coverage every Florida driver carries — Personal Injury Protection, Property Damage Liability, and typically bodily injury liability. A DUI mainly changes your required limits and adds the FR-44 filing on top; it doesn't reinvent your policy from scratch.

## License reinstatement and insurance

For many drivers, FR-44 coverage isn't just about staying compliant going forward — it's the missing piece for getting a suspended license back. Once your policy is active with the right limits, your insurer files the certificate, which typically clears the way for reinstatement alongside any other court-ordered conditions.

## DUI insurance impact at a glance

| Factor | What changes |
|---|---|
| Required coverage | Higher liability limits (FR-44) |
| Filing period | Typically 3 years, continuous |
| Premium | Increases, varies significantly by carrier |
| Core coverage types | Unchanged (PIP, PDL, liability) |
| Carrier options | Limited to FR-44-eligible insurers |

## The role of your driving record before the DUI

Insurers don't evaluate a DUI in a vacuum. A driver with an otherwise spotless record and one isolated conviction often gets treated somewhat differently than a driver with a longer history of tickets or accidents leading up to the DUI. This doesn't change your FR-44 requirement itself, but it can meaningfully affect which carriers offer you the most competitive pricing — another reason a broad comparison matters more than settling for whichever quote arrives first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does every DUI in Florida require FR-44?** Most do, though your specific court or FLHSMV notice is the definitive source for your case.

**How long will my rate stay elevated?** Typically for your required filing period, though many drivers see gradual improvement as the conviction becomes more distant.

**Can I switch insurers after a DUI?** Yes, as long as your new policy and filing start before your old one ends, to avoid a lapse.

**Is a DUI permanently on my insurance record?** The underlying conviction stays on your driving record long-term, but its weight in pricing generally decreases once your FR-44 period ends and time passes.

**What's the single biggest mistake people make after a DUI?** Accepting the first quote without comparing others — FR-44 pricing swings enough between carriers that this alone often costs people hundreds of dollars a year.
